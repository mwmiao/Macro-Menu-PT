# Product photos

Photos must already be clean product shots from the chain's own sites: only the product, on a real transparent background, with no headlines, badges, slogans, people or decoration. Photos are not edited, with ONE owner-approved exception (2026-11-22): the chain's own photo on a plain background may have that background removed with `tools/cutout.py`, only when the cut-out passed review on dark, light and checkerboard backgrounds (no halo, nothing of the product lost); such entries are recorded in the manifest as `edited: "cutout"`, the untouched original stays in `tools/image-originals/`, and nothing else (retouching, reconstruction, generating, painting out text/badges) is allowed. With owner approval (2026-11-22) a local AI model may also (a) cut the product out of the chain's own photo and (b) erase headlines/badges/logos that lie on empty background (`tools/ai_edit.py`), only when the result passed review against the original on dark, light and checkerboard backgrounds; such entries are recorded as `edited: "ai"` (`ai_inpaint: true` when text was erased); with owner approval (2026-11-22), where a chain's own photo is cropped by the photo frame, covered by a hand, or has a badge or text over the product, a local generative inpainting model (`tools/gen_edit.py`) may complete or repair ONLY the missing or covered area: the visible original pixels are kept unchanged, the result must pass review against the original, and at most 40 % of the product's pixels may be generated; such entries are recorded as `edited: "gen"` with `gen_model`, `gen_seed`, `gen_prompt` and `gen_fraction` in the manifest; changing the item itself (different ingredients, size or quantity) is not allowed; the original always stays in `tools/image-originals/`. An unsuitable Portuguese photo is replaced by the same product from another country's official site (recorded as an `equivalent` match, which never changes the item's name, nutrition or portion); if there is none, the chain's own unsuitable photo is shown anyway with status `fallback` (items with no photo at all show the drawn placeholder), and the item is listed in `tools/image-review.md` until a clean source is found. For branded packaged goods (beer, soft drinks, water, juice, sauces/dips sold by brand) a clean transparent product shot from the brand's own site or a supermarket's product page is also acceptable when the restaurant chain has none; record it with `match: "equivalent"` and `region` set to the source (e.g. `brand-pt`, `continente-pt`, `brand-es`). Edits follow the rules above.

Pipeline: `tools/image-sources/<chain>.json` maps each item's Portuguese name to an image URL (or `{url, region, match}`). `node tools/fetch-images.mjs [chain…]` downloads new originals (needs `ffmpeg`) into `tools/image-originals/<chain>/<sha1(url)[0:12]>.webp` and records them in `tools/image-manifest.json` (source, region, match, items, status `pending`/`approved`/`fallback`/`rejected`, issue). After checking a photo by eye (`python tools/image-sheet.py <chain>` draws a contact sheet on a checkerboard), set its status. `python tools/process-images.py` (needs `pillow numpy`) then trims the transparent margin of approved photos, saves them to `images/<chain>/` named by a hash of their content (so a changed photo always gets a new URL), regenerates `js/data/images.js` and writes the review list. `python tools/cutout.py` creates cut-out candidates, `--sheet` draws review sheets. `tools/.venv-ai/Scripts/python.exe tools/ai_edit.py` (virtual environment `tools/.venv-ai`, git-ignored) does the AI cut-out and text removal, see its header. `tools/.venv-ai/Scripts/python.exe tools/gen_edit.py` (same virtual environment) completes or repairs cropped or covered photos with a local inpainting model, see its header. Originals stay in `tools/`, which is not copied into the Docker image.

mcdonalds.pt refuses scripted downloads, so McDonald's photos are fetched in a browser on the site's product pages (www.mcdonalds.pt/#produtos), converted to WebP there and saved as `tools/image-originals/mcdonalds/<id>.webp`, where `<id>` is the first 12 hex characters of the SHA-1 of the image URL. The fetch script then indexes them without downloading.
